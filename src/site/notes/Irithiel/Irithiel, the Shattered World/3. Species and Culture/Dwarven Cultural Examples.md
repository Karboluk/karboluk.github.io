# Class Examples

Here are some examples of how a hero of any given class may, or may not, fit into dwarven society.

## Magical Classes

Dwarves, despite their reputation to the contrary, are inherently a magical people. Their culture is ripe with the gifts of the gods

### Bard
#### Entertainer
Dwarves in Irithiel celebrate well, and often. A good song, a touching poem, or simply a beat to dance to- all forms of art are welcome. [[Irithiel/Irithiel, the Shattered World/5. Religions and Deities/Cultural Religious Practices/The Dwarven Pantheon\|Hanseath]] has decreed it so, and too-bloody-right it shall be.

#### Sounder
Dwarven Sounders use specialized calls, taught to their people by [[Irithiel/Irithiel, the Shattered World/5. Religions and Deities/Deities/Major Deities/Thalassus of the Oceans\|Dumathoin]], to see what lies beyond the walls of stone around them. As an action, a Sounder may spend one use of their Stonecunning ability to gain a sense of the current dimensions and composition of natural stone, metal or gem formations within 300'. The call is loud and vibrates through the stone, and is audible up to 500' away, even through total cover comprised of the above material. This ability does not function with worked stone, or other solid materials such as wood or glass; any vegetation or liquids that may be present does not interfere with it. 


The Sounder automatically learns of any dimensional attributes, such as thickness of walls, tunnels, and pockets or cavities that are not stone, metal or gem. Areas within the range that are composed of materials that Sounding can not penetrate are automatically identified as such, but it is not possible to determine their composition. As part of the action, the Sounder may attempt a DC 12 intelligence (Investigation) check to gain specifics of the composition of the stone, metal or gem, and whether any cavities are air filled, water filled or lava filled. Though this ability gives the current state of the sensed terrain, the Sounder also gains tremorsense 60' for 10 minutes, as per the normal Stonecunning ability.

#### Spy
When a dwarven society is in proximity with, and has at least neutral relations with, a human or human-like society, it isn't uncommon for dwarves with an interested in the social arts to go abroad and bring back tales, information and/or exotic materials as part of trade missions. When this isn't possible, or when a more delicate touch is required, they can become spies. Each spy will operate according to their own strengths; posing as a bodyguard, a mercenary, a craftsman, an entertainer, a servant - whatever cover will make their presence believable - dwarven spies have the tenacity and patience, charm and guile, to truly crack any defenses in all but the most paranoid human kingdoms. 

### Cleric
Clerics are very common among the Dwarves, due to their cultural adherence to [[Irithiel/Irithiel, the Shattered World/5. Religions and Deities/Cultural Religious Practices/The Dwarven Pantheon\|The Dwarven Pantheon]]. Clerics may follow any walk of life, as the various gods each oversee disparate areas of activity.  Any profession, including those described in the other class sections, could be a cleric- except the Battlerager.

### Druid
Druidic magic is known to the dwarves, but isn't widespread. Not ignored like arcane pursuits, traditional plant and animal-centered druidic practice instead occurs most often in small settlements; commonly surface towns or long-term outposts underground, where interfacing with nature in its wild form happens. 


#### Crystal Shepherds
But then there are crystals.

Crystalline growth, very much a natural process, is highly prized among dwarves. In large cities and mountainhomes, it is not uncommon to have farms growing various crystals of different colours and compositions, for decorative or functional use. This activity is overseen by [[Irithiel/Irithiel, the Shattered World/5. Religions and Deities/Cultural Religious Practices/The Dwarven Pantheon\|Dumathoin]], the Lord of the Depths, and little distinction is made between these druids and his clerics. The same druids also tend to make themselves useful in other areas, such as encouraging mushroom farming or making sure the city has enough cats, lizards, or other vermin-hunting pets to maintain cleanliness.


### Sorcerers, Wizards, Warlocks
The dwarves do not shun arcane magics, nor are they unaware of them. They simply have no use for them, as their gods will provide additional instruction as needed. It is therefore very rare for a dwarf to turn to one of these pursuits; though, of course, it happens once in a great while.

#### Sorcerer
Magical bloodlines run thin among the dwarves, but a divine soul sorcerer will emerge from time to time. Largely indistinguishable from the other faithful, they spend their life as any dwarf would.

#### Wizards
In extreme circumstances, where a Clan has been displaced or decimated, you may see a dwarf pursue wizardry. Perhaps a young dwarf, trying very hard to improve the lot of his family after a famine has reduced the clan below what's needed for long-term survival. Perhaps a refugee in human or elven lands, who wants to learn what they can while they are stuck, to bring home. Perhaps a dwarf who, for whatever reason, finds themself unemployable among their own people- this is extremely rare, and will cause a dwarf to go mad over time- so fills their time in arcane pursuits in desperation or spite. 

Each wizard is an exception, so there is no "normal" societal place.

#### Warlocks
A dwarf who has taken a deal is either shunned, pitied, or helped; depending on the circumstances that caused the situation. Was it willing? Was it forced? Was it imposed? The dwarven gods have been known to intercede with the latter, but otherwise contracting with a higher power outside of the pantheon is one of the few grounds for the ultimate punishment: exile.

So...any that remain are surely keeping it hidden. Why? What value do they think it will bring to the Clan? It must be great, to risk such a breach of trust.

## Martial Classes


### Fighters
Fighters are the rank-and-file guard and warriors of any dwarven settlement. Sentries at the gate? Fighters. Instructors at the barracks? Fighters. Crossbow dwarves on the battlements? Fighters. 

Whether they specialize in strength of arms, deadly accuracy, or survivability; other than commoners, fighters are certainly the most common archetype that dwarves fall into.

#### Guardsmen
Dwarves don't have a great need to guard against thieves and pickpockets in their own settlements, as that sort of activity is exceedingly rare; but dwarves trading or mingling with human societies certainly do. Sharp eyed, quick footed, good at reading intent and subduing foes non-lethally, a dwarven guardsman takes his job seriously while happily assisting and interfacing with honest folk. In the mountainhome, guardsmen typically work as tunnel guards in excavation or mining operations. 

In urban settings, guardsmen are armed with blunt weapons; truncheons (clubs), maces and hammers; and with more exotic tools such as nets, manacles or polearms in stranger or more perilous situations.

In caves, or in outposts above and below ground, they will be armed with weapons suitable to the terrain and the opposition they expect. Ranged options to thin and slow down an attack, or dissuade animals, giants and other wild threats; axes and swords to hew them down once the enemy closes.

#### Soldiers
The dwarves of Irithiel do not often go to war, thanks to [[Irithiel/Irithiel, the Shattered World/5. Religions and Deities/Cultural Religious Practices/The Dwarven Pantheon\|Clangeddin]]'s teachings about preemptive defense, but when they have, a more organized and co-ordinated infantry cannot be found outside of Hobgoblin lands. A shield wall, patterned to become dizzying as the enemy charges in, pikes behind to spear cavalry; bolts and stones raining down from above from ranged and siege units whose calculations are deadly accurate. Implacable, unmovable, and clad in the finest armor, dwarven soldiers are a sight to behold- inspiring or terrifying, depending on one's perspective.



### Barbarian

There are those who take toughness seriously, and then those who **take. toughness. seriously.** As diligently as with any art, some dwarves have perfected the art of being stabbed and not going down. 

#### Battleragers and Battleborn

**Battleragers**, Dwarven Shock troops, are part of an army for two main reasons:
- They rush the enemy, and give the shield wall time to assemble. Dwarven victory is likely.
- They rush the enemy, and any sensible enemy runs away. Dwarven victory is assured. 

**Battleborn**, those divinely blessed by [[Irithiel/Irithiel, the Shattered World/5. Religions and Deities/Cultural Religious Practices/The Dwarven Pantheon\|Clangeddin]], channel and direct their friends into maximal effectiveness. 

##### Battleragers
Wearing bladed, hooked, or spiked armor, the Battlerager hurts you simply by getting close, but he doesn't stop there. He doesn't notice if has has an arrow sticking out of him. He doesn't notice if his ear has been taken off. He doesn't notice that he's hacking into the corpse of someone he killed five axe blows ago. This frenzy isn't anger; this is divinely granted ecstasy- the pain simply isn't there. 

Exalted warriors and highly respected in the community, when outside of war Battleragers are given wider berth from most folks, as casual fighting- usually fun and mostly harmless- takes a different turn in their case. They are usually assigned duties outside of the mountainhome that allow them to vent off energy by clearing out vermin in the mines- giant spiders, small dragons, devils. The small stuff. 

##### Battleborn
Whereas Battleragers are simply berserk, Battleborn carry divine blessing. Their strikes land with the power of a god, and their presence on the battlefield alone can change the course of history. Battleborn tend to vary in their skillset- some focusing on toughness and tenacity (Barbarians), and others focusing their divine blessings (Paladins). Both are equally effective, and equally fearsome.

#### Forge Hands
It's easy to think of toughness as the warrior's purview, but standing in front of a crucible heated by lava, the metallic skin of the Smelter reflecting even more heat while he works his divine creations, keeping the tool blank steady and rotating it in his rhythm; this is toughness as well. 

Constant, patient, unwilting resilience, is the hallmark of a Forgehand; that, and the thick callouses they have from working hot metal. A very meditative state, the Forgehand's focus grants clarity and the speed and will to do what must be done, when it must be done, regardless of the cost to oneself. The work is divine, and can not be interrupted.

#### Stone Warriors
Not all who prize toughness are as mindless as the Battlerager. Where their frenzy numbs them to pain, some look to experience it; know it; endure it.

The Stone Warrior looks like a fighter on the surface, but has withstood a number of trials to harden herself.  She is the rock that her infantry unit anchors on. She is the unmoveable bodyguard that will not let you get to her charge. She is like the Stone itself. 

Stone Warriors, whether in a fighting unit, working as a guard, or escorting an important charge,  often eschew shields in exchange for more damaging weapons, as this also has the effect of making them look like easier targets than their heavily-armored comrades.


### Paladins
The Holy Warrior archetype of the Paladin is strong in dwarven culture, but slightly different than the human interpretation of that ideal. Rarely the White Knight in Shining Armor, a Dwarven Paladin is an aspirant to become as close as possible to the ideal that is the god they venerate. They have met their role model in person, possibly often, possibly training directly with them, and they want to be the same shining example to the Clan that their god is; if not quite as brightly shining.

Most Oaths can be treated as they are in the Player's Handbook, but the Oath of the Ancients should be understood to reflect a more subterranean and/or divinely crafted source and aesthetic, and the Oath of Conquest should reflect the inexorable solidity and virtue of dwarven progression instead of the tyranny described (which may be the same from a goblin's point of view, of course.)

#### Order of the Crafted
Paladins of Moradin protect the Clan from attacks from above; whether from foreign armies or from threats like dragons or giants. They are also responsible for the Clan's safety in the even of migration. Oaths of Devotion, of Glory and of the Ancients are the most common.

#### Order of the Mountainhome
Paladins of Berronar protect the Clan from internal strife, and serve as judges or arbiters in the case of intractable disagreement. Oaths of Devotion, of the Crown, and of Redemption are the most common.

#### Order of the Deepguard
Paladins of Dumathoin protect the Clan from attacks from below, which often involves detecting and warding off eldritch beings. Oaths of the Ancient and the Watchers are the most common.

#### Order of the Battleborn
Paladins of Clangeddin serve in war, often as [[Irithiel/Irithiel, the Shattered World/3. Species and Culture/Dwarven Cultural Examples#Battleborn\|#Battleborn]]. Oaths of Glory or Vengeance are the most common, and Oaths of Conquest have been sworn in the past.

#### Order of the Celebrant
Paladins of Hanseath share the good news, spread joy, and try to bridge gaps caused by disagreement. Oaths of the Ancients and Glory are the most common.



### Monks

#### Brawling
As implied above, casual fighting is not only legal among dwarves, but is fairly common; it's used as a method for settling disputes, for demonstrating skill, and sometime just for blowing off steam. Non-lethal fighting is thought by the Elders to produce a stronger Clan of dwarves, and is essentially free training for those who are not already in a martial field. 

So while monks, in the human or elven "meditation and calm" sense, are fairly rare, brawling unarmed fighters are reasonably common. Way of the Open Hand and Way of the Drunken Fist are well known to the Clan.


#### Resilience
Since the settling of the Shining Vale, the establishment of the human Kingdom, and the canonization of their Saints, a rare migration of human ideals has begun to pass back to the dwarven Clan there. The priests of St. Kor have begun espousing the Way of Mithril Skin, an unarmed fighting philosophy that focuses not on evasiveness, but on the virtues of resilience. unsurprisingly, this has resonated among many dwarves. Though the practice is more common in the portion of the Clan that lives near the Cathedral in Corwyn City, there are those in the Mountainhome that have begun to practice it as well.


## Specialist Classes
Dwarven adventurers, soldiers and other uncommon folk are always skilled- whether it's their profession, a crafting trade or more commonly both, expressions of skill are valued almost as much as the Clan. Dedicated specialists therefore naturally find themselves among the more active folk in the Clan as well.
### Rangers
Whether on the surface or underground, there is always a need for those who can track or shadow threats, watchposts to be manned, and natural threats to be identified before they escalate. 

#### Watchers
Border Guards, Wall Guards or Cavern Guards, Watchers make sure that anything out of the ordinary is noted, and dealt with if possible. The first line of defense, warning the Clan if trouble is incoming that they can't deal with, Watchers are probably the Dwarves who spend the most time away from home. This has led to estrangement, resentment and cultural drift in the past, so the Elders, particularly the Priests of Berronar, go out of their way to make sure any Watchers are well taken care of. Gift baskets from home, new equipment, well wishes and celebrations when they return keep a Watcher tied to their family.

#### Finders
Rumours are often gathered from other civilizations, or from the gods themselves, and someone has to check them out. The role of the Finder is just that- voyage off into the wild, an expedition to determine the facts, location, and/or nature of anything the elders feel is important. It could be a new mine, a new threat, or a new home.

#### Hunters
Where Finders to to determine the nature of a threat, and Watchers report threats close to home, Hunters go to end it. A party of Dwarven Hunters, pets and friends in tow, can clear out anything from a nest of goblins to a large dragon, if they are careful enough. Sometimes, they clear out an enemy town; but action like this would be looked at as an option of last resort.

### Rogues
Though pickpockets, burglars and con artists are uncommon in the Mountainhome, those are still skills to be honed and arts to perfect. A person who may have become a criminal in a human kingdom is a well-compensated professional in the Clan.

#### Locksmiths
Every part wants a rogue who can pick a lock, or so the common wisdom goes. Well what about one who picks them so well because she makes them? A locksmith is a mechanically-minded person with an eye for small details, small machines and potential hazards. It is quite common for Dwarven Locksmiths, once trained up at home, to venture out into the world to see what other folk craft to protect their valuables; the best way to make sure foreign thieves can't break into your vault is to see how theirs are crafted, after all, traps and all.

#### Scouts
Working with Watchers, Finders and Hunters, with Armies and with Spies, a good scout is invaluable. Someone who can get the lay of the land with no-one being the wiser can give you the intelligence you need to deploy the correct response. The correct response means a better and cleaner result, and less danger to the Dwarves that were sent.

#### Ratcatchers
The Clan guards its home with zeal, jealousy and no small amount of ferocity. They know that they have what others want. Thus, every large settlement will have a few Ratcatchers; a euphemistic name that gets laughs from foreigners who don't just don't get the joke. The rats dwarves care about are big. Goblins sized, human sized, sometimes bigger. Standing upright, and with sticky fingers. There are always stories about human troublemakers bragging in the local tavern, saying they're going to get one over on "those greedy dwarves", who just don't come home from  a trip one day. You can thank the Ratcatchers.
